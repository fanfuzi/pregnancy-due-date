Pregnancy Test Accuracy: When to Take a Test for Best Results

By the Pregnancy Calculator Hub editorial team · Updated September 4, 2026

This guide is for general information only and does not replace advice from your doctor or midwife.

Getting a false negative can be confusing and frustrating. Here's exactly when and how to take a pregnancy test for the most accurate result.

Pregnancy tests have become incredibly accurate in recent years, but knowing when to take a test is just as important as which brand you buy. Taking a test too early is the #1 cause of false negatives—the most common mistake women make when trying to conceive or suspecting pregnancy.

How Pregnancy Tests Work

Home pregnancy tests detect hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), a hormone produced by the placenta after a fertilized egg implants in the uterus. hCG levels double approximately every 48-72 hours in early pregnancy.

The key to accuracy is hCG concentration. The more concentrated your urine, the more likely the test will detect even tiny amounts of hCG. This is why timing and urine type matter so much.

The Best Time to Take a Pregnancy Test

Rule #1: Wait Until Your Missed Period

The most accurate results come from testing on or after the first day of your missed period. By this point, if you're pregnant, hCG levels are usually high enough to be detected by even basic tests.

Testing Before Your Missed Period (Early Testing)

Some "early result" or "early response" tests claim to detect pregnancy 5-6 days before your missed period. These can work, but:

  • Accuracy drops significantly when testing early
  • False negatives are much more common
  • Even if the test says "not pregnant," you still might be—just need more time
  • Recommended only if you're actively trying to conceive and tracking ovulation

When to Test After a Late Period

If your period is late but you got a negative result:

  • Wait 3-5 days and test again
  • hCG may still be too low to detect immediately after implantation
  • Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation and your period
  • Some women have naturally irregular cycles

First Morning Urine vs. Any Time of Day

For the most accurate result, test with your first morning urine. This is the most concentrated sample, containing the highest hCG levels.

Testing later in the day is less reliable because:

  • Diluted urine (from drinking water, coffee, etc.) lowers hCG concentration
  • hCG may be below the test's detection threshold
  • You're more likely to get a faint line or false negative

Exception: If your hCG levels are very high (later in pregnancy, or with multiples), any time of day works fine.

Pregnancy Test Accuracy: By the Numbers

Most modern pregnancy tests claim 99% accuracy when used correctly. But what does that really mean?

When Used on Day of Missed Period

  • True positive rate: ~99% of pregnant women will test positive
  • False negative rate: ~1% (usually testing too early)
  • False positive rate: Very rare (<1%)

When Used 5-6 Days Before Missed Period

  • True positive rate: Drops to ~50-75% depending on brand
  • False negative rate: High—25-50% of pregnancies will be missed
  • Best for: Women actively tracking ovulation and trying to conceive

What Causes a False Negative?

A false negative means the test says "not pregnant" when you actually are pregnant. Common causes:

  1. Testing too early: hCG hasn't built up enough yet (the #1 cause)
  2. Diluted urine: Testing later in the day instead of first morning
  3. Expired test: Check the expiration date on the box
  4. Not following instructions: Using too much or too little urine, waiting too long to read results
  5. Ectopic pregnancy: hCG may rise more slowly; tests may stay negative longer
  6. Fertility medications: Some contain hCG and can cause false positives (not negatives)

What Causes a False Positive?

False positives are much rarer but can happen due to:

  • Chemical pregnancy: Very early loss; technically pregnant but not viable
  • Recent miscarriage: hCG can remain in your system for 2-4 weeks
  • Fertility medications: hCG injections (trigger shot) can cause false positive for up to 14 days
  • Evaporation lines: If you read the test after the recommended time (usually 5-10 minutes), a faint line may appear—but it's not a positive result

How to Read a Pregnancy Test Accurately

It sounds simple, but many people misread tests:

  • Read within the time window: Usually 3-5 minutes (check the instructions)
  • Two lines = positive: Even if the test line is faint, it counts as positive
  • One line = negative: Only the control line shows
  • No lines at all: The test may be faulty; try again with a new one
  • Faint line after 10 minutes: Ignore it—likely evaporation line
  • If unsure: Take another test in 2-3 days or visit your doctor

Blood vs. Urine Tests

For the most definitive answer, a blood test at your doctor's office is superior to a home test:

  • Qualitative hCG test: Simply tells you yes/no, same as home test
  • Quantitative hCG test: Measures exact hCG levels; can detect pregnancy 6-8 days after ovulation
  • More sensitive: Can detect lower hCG levels than home tests
