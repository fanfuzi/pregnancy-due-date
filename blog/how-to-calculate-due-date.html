How to Calculate Your Due Date: The Complete Guide

Last updated: January 2024

Finding out you're pregnant is one of life's most exciting moments. One of the first questions every expecting parent asks is: "When is my due date?" This comprehensive guide will walk you through every method to calculate your due date accurately.

Why Due Dates Matter

Your due date helps you and your healthcare provider:

  • Schedule prenatal appointments at the right times
  • Prepare for labor and delivery
  • Track fetal development milestones
  • Plan maternity leave and baby preparations

Method 1: Last Menstrual Period (LMP) - Most Common

The most widely used method is Naegele's Rule. It's simple:

  1. Take the first day of your last menstrual period
  2. Add 7 days
  3. Subtract 3 months

Example: If your last period started on January 1st:

  • January 1st + 7 days = January 8th
  • January 8th - 3 months = October 8th
  • Your due date: October 8th

Important: This method assumes a 28-day cycle. If your cycles are longer or shorter, your actual due date may vary by several days.

Method 2: Conception Date

If you know the exact date of conception (typically 14 days after your LMP), you can calculate by:

  1. Take the conception date
  2. Add 266 days (38 weeks)

This method is more accurate if you tracked ovulation precisely, such as with an ovulation predictor kit.

Method 3: IVF Transfer Date

For pregnancies achieved through In Vitro Fertilization (IVF), the calculation is different based on embryo age:

  • 3-day embryo (Cleavage stage): Add 266 days to transfer date
  • 5-day embryo (Blastocyst): Add 261 days to transfer date

IVF due dates are often known very precisely, making them more accurate than LMP estimates.

Factors That Affect Due Date Accuracy

Remember: Your due date is an estimate, not a deadline. Only about 4% of babies are born on their exact due date. Factors that influence accuracy include:

  • Irregular periods: If you have irregular cycles, LMP dating is less accurate
  • First pregnancy: First-time mothers often deliver later than calculated
  • Ultrasound dating: A first-trimester ultrasound (11-14 weeks) can correct the due date by +/- 5-7 days
  • Genetics: Family history of timing can influence when you deliver

How Accurate Are Due Date Calculators?

Due date calculators using LMP are accurate within 7-10 days for women with regular 28-day cycles. If your cycles vary significantly, the window of error increases.

The most accurate method is a first-trimester ultrasound, which can confirm or adjust your due date based on actual fetal measurements (crown-rump length).
