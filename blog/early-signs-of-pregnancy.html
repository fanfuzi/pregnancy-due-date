How to Tell if You're Pregnant: Early Signs & Symptoms Before a Missed Period

By the Pregnancy Calculator Hub editorial team · Updated September 4, 2026

This guide is for general information only and does not replace advice from your doctor or midwife.

A missed period is the most obvious sign, but many women notice other clues days or even weeks before. Here's what to look for.

You suspect you might be pregnant, but you're not sure. Maybe your period is late, or maybe you're noticing subtle changes in your body. While a pregnancy test is the only definitive answer, there are early signs of pregnancy that can appear as soon as one to two weeks after conception.

This guide covers every early symptom, when they typically appear, and what they mean for your pregnancy timeline. If you're trying to conceive or had an unexpected late period, this is the article to read.

The Very First Signs of Pregnancy

Some symptoms appear before your missed period—sometimes just 6-8 days after conception. These are caused by the fertilized egg implanting in the uterus and the surge of pregnancy hormones (hCG, progesterone, estrogen).

1. Implantation Bleeding or Spotting

About 25-30% of pregnant women experience light spotting around the time of implantation, which occurs 6-12 days after conception. It's often mistaken for a light period.

How to tell the difference from a period:

  • Color: Pink or brown (old blood), not bright red
  • Amount: Light spotting, not heavy flow
  • Duration: 1-2 days, not 3-7 days
  • Timing: Occurs before your expected period

2. Breast Tenderness and Swelling

Your breasts may feel tender, swollen, or tingly as early as 1-2 weeks after conception. This is one of the earliest and most common signs. You may notice:

  • Breasts feel heavy, full, or sensitive to touch
  • Areolas (the area around nipples) may darken
  • Veins on breasts may become more visible

3. Fatigue

Sudden, overwhelming tiredness is a hallmark early sign. Your body is working hard to build the placenta and support embryonic development. Progesterone, which rises rapidly after conception, has a sedating effect. You may find yourself needing naps or going to bed hours earlier than usual.

4. Frequent Urination

Starting around week 6-8, you may notice yourself running to the bathroom more often. This is caused by increased blood volume and the growing uterus pressing on the bladder. It's one of the reasons pregnancy is often detected before a test—you're simply more aware of your body's signals.

5. Nausea (Morning Sickness)

Despite the name, morning sickness can strike any time of day. It typically begins around week 4-6 and may be one of the first symptoms you notice. Early nausea is caused by rising hCG levels. You might feel:

  • Queasiness or "butterflies" in the stomach
  • Aversion to certain smells (coffee, meat, perfume)
  • Increased salivation

6. Bloating and Cramping

You might experience mild cramping (similar to menstrual cramps) and bloating in early pregnancy. These are caused by hormonal changes and the uterus beginning to expand. While mild cramping is normal, severe pain should be evaluated.

7. Mood Swings

Hormonal surges can make you feel emotional, irritable, or tearful. Mood swings are common in the first trimester and often improve by the second. If you find yourself crying at a commercial or snapping at your partner over something trivial, hormones may be the culprit.

8. Food Aversions or Cravings

Suddenly can't stand the smell of your favorite food? That's normal. Food aversions are thought to be a protective mechanism—your body steering you away from potentially harmful substances. Cravings (pickles and ice cream, anyone?) are equally common and equally mysterious in origin.

9. Headaches

Hormonal changes and increased blood volume can trigger mild headaches in early pregnancy. Stay hydrated and rest when possible. If headaches are severe or persistent, consult your doctor.

10. Slight Rise in Basal Body Temperature

If you track your basal body temperature (BBT) for fertility, you may notice it stays elevated beyond your expected period. BBT rises after ovulation and stays high if you're pregnant. This is a subtle sign that requires consistent tracking to notice.

When Early Signs Typically Appear

Here's a timeline of when symptoms typically appear relative to your last menstrual period (LMP) and expected period:

Implantation bleeding6-12 days after conception
Breast tenderness1-2 weeks after conception
Fatigue1 week after conception
NauseaUsually from around week 6 (about 2 weeks after a missed period)
Frequent urinationWeek 6-8
Food aversions/cravingsWeek 4-6

When Symptoms Are Warning Signs

Most early pregnancy symptoms are normal, but some warrant a call to your doctor:

  • Heavy bleeding — Soaking a pad in an hour (possible miscarriage)
  • Severe cramping — Not mild twinges, but sharp or constant pain (possible ectopic pregnancy)
  • Severe vomiting — Can't keep food or water down for 24+ hours (hyperemesis gravidarum)
  • High fever — Over 100.4°F/38°C (infection risk)
  • Severe headache with vision changes — Possible preeclampsia (rare in early pregnancy but serious)
  • Painful urination or back pain — Could indicate UTI

How to Know for Sure: Take a Test

Symptoms are clues, but only a pregnancy test can confirm pregnancy. Home urine tests detect hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), a hormone produced after implantation.

When to test:

  • Wait until the first day of your missed period for the most accurate result
  • Some sensitive tests can detect hCG 5-6 days before your missed period
  • Take the test with your first morning urine (most concentrated hCG)
  • If negative but period still late, wait 3-5 days and test again

For a more definitive answer, visit your doctor for a blood test, which can detect pregnancy earlier and more accurately than a urine test.
