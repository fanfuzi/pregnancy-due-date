What to Pack in Your Hospital Bag: Complete Checklist

Don't get caught off guard. Pack your hospital bag by week 35 with this essential checklist for labor, delivery, and postpartum recovery.

When you're 35+ weeks pregnant, there's one important task that's easy to procrastinate: packing your hospital bag. But when labor starts—whether at 37 weeks or 42 weeks—you'll be glad you had everything ready. This checklist covers everything you need for mom, partner, and baby, plus hospital essentials many first-time parents forget.

When to Pack Your Hospital Bag

Aim to have your bags packed by week 35. Put one bag by the door or in your car. Some parents keep a "go-bag" in the car from week 37 onward, just in case labor progresses quickly.

For Mom: During Labor & Delivery

Comfort & Essentials

  • ID and insurance card — The hospital will ask immediately
  • Birth plan — If you have one, print 2-3 copies
  • Phone charger (long cord) — Hospital outlets can be far from beds
  • Lip balm — Hospitals are very dry; your lips will chap
  • Hair ties / headband — Keep hair off your face during labor
  • Warm socks / non-slip socks — Hospital floors are cold
  • Robe or cardigan — Hospitals are chilly; robes are cozy for visitors too
  • Eye mask — For sleeping between contractions at night
  • Ear plugs / white noise app — Hospital halls are noisy

Pain Relief & Snacks

  • Hard candies or lollipops — Keep energy up during long labors
  • Ice chips / sports drinks — If you're NPO (nothing by mouth) for potential surgery
  • Ginger chews or tea bags — If you get nauseous
  • Massage tool / tennis ball — For back labor or partner to use
  • Essential oils (lavender, peppermint) — Check hospital policy first

For Mom: Postpartum Recovery

After delivery, you'll need comfort items for yourself. Many first-time moms underestimate postpartum discomfort:

  • High-waisted underwear (3-4 pairs) — Disposable or old cotton; you'll bleed for 4-6 weeks
  • Maxi pads (heavy/overnight) — Bring more than you think; you'll go through a lot
  • Perineal spray (witch hazel) — Soothes discomfort after vaginal birth
  • Perineal cold packs — Hospitals usually provide these, but bring extras
  • Stool softener / fiber supplements — Postpartum constipation is common
  • Nipple cream (lanolin) — If breastfeeding, this is a lifesaver
  • Nursing bras / tank tops (2-3) — Front-opening for easy access
  • Going-home outfit — Maternity clothes; you won't fit into pre-pregnancy jeans yet
  • Bathrobe and slippers — For walking the halls postpartum
  • Toiletries bag — Toothbrush, deodorant, face wash, dry shampoo, hairbrush

For the Birth Partner / Support Person

Your partner or support person will be with you for hours (or days). Don't forget their needs:

  • ID and insurance info — They'll need to check in too
  • Change of clothes — Labor can last 12+ hours; bring comfy layers
  • Toiletries — Toothbrush, deodorant, phone charger
  • Cash and cards — For vending machines or parking
  • Snacks and water bottle — They'll be running on adrenaline
  • Entertainment — Book, tablet, or downloaded shows for the lulls

For Baby

The hospital provides diapers, wipes, blankets, and basic supplies. But you may want a few personal items:

  • Going-home outfit — Newborn or 0-3 months; consider weather
  • Carseat (installed) — You cannot leave without one
  • Swaddle blankets (2-3) — Hospital blankets are thin
  • Hat and mittens — Newborns lose heat quickly
  • pacifier — If you plan to use one (bring a few)

Hospital Paperwork & Admin

  • Photo ID and insurance card — For both parents
  • Birth plan copies — Give one to your nurse, one to your doctor
  • Contraction timer app — Or a notebook and pen for tracking
  • Pediatrician's contact info — The hospital will notify them

What NOT to Pack

Avoid these common mistakes:

  • Too many outfits for baby — Hospital provides onesies; you won't need multiples
  • Valuables — Jewelry, expensive electronics, large amounts of cash
  • Too much makeup — You'll be exhausted and sweaty
  • Full-size toiletries — Stick to travel sizes
  • Books / work laptop — This is not the time to catch up on emails

Packing Timeline

Break packing into small tasks so it doesn't feel overwhelming:

  1. Week 30: Buy any missing items (nursing bras, perineal spray)
  2. Week 33: Pack essentials for mom (ID, toiletries, postpartum pads)
  3. Week 35: Pack going-home outfits and finalize baby items
  4. Week 37: Put bags by the door or in the car

Recommended Hospital Bag Products on Amazon

If you need to pick up any of these items, here are highly-rated options:

  • Disposable postpartum underwear — More comfortable than pads for many women
  • Lanolin nipple cream — Lansinoh is the most popular brand
  • Witch hazel pads (Tucks) — Soothing for hemorrhoids and perineal discomfort
  • Donut pillow / donut seat cushion — Helps with sitting pain after vaginal birth
