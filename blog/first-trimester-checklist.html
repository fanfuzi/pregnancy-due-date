First Trimester Checklist: What to Do in Weeks 1-12

Your complete guide to navigating the exciting (and sometimes overwhelming) first trimester of pregnancy.

The first trimester covers weeks 1 through 12 of pregnancy. It's a time of enormous change—your body is building a placenta and supporting rapid fetal development. Many women experience morning sickness, fatigue, and emotional swings. This checklist will help you stay on track and make the most of this critical period.

Medical Tasks

Confirm Your Pregnancy

Take a home pregnancy test after a missed period, or better yet, visit your doctor for a blood test that detects hCG earlier and more accurately.

Schedule Your First Prenatal Appointment

Book your first prenatal visit around 8 weeks gestation. This visit typically includes:

  • Confirmation of pregnancy via ultrasound
  • Due date estimation
  • Medical history review
  • Blood work (CBC, blood type, Rh factor, STI screening)
  • Urine test for protein and glucose

Start Prenatal Vitamins

If you're not already taking them, start a prenatal vitamin with 400-800 mcg of folic acid immediately. Folic acid prevents neural tube defects and is crucial in the first few weeks—often before you even know you're pregnant.

Discuss Medications with Your Doctor

Review all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies you take. Some are safe, but others (like certain acne medications, anti-seizure drugs, or blood thinners) need adjustment.

Lifestyle Adjustments

Quit Smoking and Alcohol

If you smoke or drink, now is the time to stop. Even small amounts of alcohol or nicotine can harm fetal development. Ask your doctor about cessation programs if needed.

Limit Caffeine

Most studies suggest limiting caffeine to 200mg per day (about one 12-oz cup of coffee). Switch to decaf or herbal teas if you're a heavy coffee drinker.

Eat Nutritiously

Focus on whole foods: lean proteins, leafy greens, whole grains, and healthy fats. If you experience morning sickness, small frequent meals and ginger can help manage nausea.

Exercise (If You Were Active Before)

If you already exercised, you can usually continue with moderate activity. Avoid contact sports, scuba diving, or activities with fall risk. Start with walking, prenatal yoga, or swimming if you were sedentary before pregnancy.

Emotional & Mental Health

Expect Mood Swings

Hormones are surging. You might feel euphoric one moment and tearful the next. This is completely normal. Communicate with your partner about your needs.

Consider a Support Group

Joining a local or online pregnancy group can provide emotional support and answer questions. Reddit's r/pregnancy is a popular community, but always verify medical advice with your doctor.

Planning & Preparation

Choose a Healthcare Provider

Decide between an OB-GYN, midwife, or family doctor. Consider factors like hospital affiliation, insurance coverage, and your birthing preferences.

Budget for Maternity Costs

Review your health insurance to understand what prenatal care, delivery, and postpartum care will cost. Consider opening a Health Savings Account (HSA) if available.

Announce When You're Ready

Many couples wait until after the first trimester (12 weeks) when miscarriage risk drops significantly. Announcing early is personal choice, but 12 weeks is the most common milestone.

Warning Signs to Watch

Contact your doctor immediately if you experience:

  • Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  • Heavy bleeding (soaking a pad in an hour)
  • Severe nausea or vomiting that prevents you from keeping fluids down
  • High fever (over 100.4F/38C)
  • Painful urination or back pain

Recommended Products for the First Trimester

  • Prenatal vitamins with DHA - essential for brain development
  • Ginger chews or tea - natural morning sickness relief
  • Supportive maternity bras - breast tenderness starts early
  • Body pillow - helps with sleep comfort as your body changes
