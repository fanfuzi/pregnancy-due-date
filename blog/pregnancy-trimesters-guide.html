Pregnancy Trimesters Explained: What to Expect in Each Stage

By the Pregnancy Calculator Hub editorial team · Updated September 4, 2026

This guide is for general information only and does not replace advice from your doctor or midwife.

A trimester-by-trimester breakdown of fetal development, maternal changes, and key milestones.

Pregnancy is typically divided into three trimesters, each lasting about 13 weeks. Understanding what happens in each stage helps you prepare for the changes ahead, recognize warning signs, and know when to seek medical care. This guide walks you through every trimester with practical advice and developmental milestones.

First Trimester: Weeks 1-12

The first trimester is a period of rapid and dramatic change—for both you and your baby. Many women don't even know they're pregnant until after week 4 or 5, making early prenatal care crucial.

Fetal Development

By the end of the first trimester, your baby has grown from a single cell to a fully formed fetus with all major organs and body systems in place:

  • Week 4: Neural tube forms (becomes brain and spine)
  • Week 6: Heart begins to beat; limb buds appear
  • Week 8: All major organs formed; embryo becomes fetus
  • Week 12: Fingers and toes separated; can make a fist

Maternal Changes

Your body is working overtime to build a placenta and nurture new life. Common first-trimester experiences include:

  • Morning sickness: Nausea and vomiting, often triggered by smells
  • Fatigue: Progesterone surges make you sleepy
  • Breast tenderness: Hormonal changes prepare breasts for lactation
  • Frequent urination: Uterus presses on bladder
  • Mood swings: Emotional fluctuations are normal

Key Actions

  1. Confirm pregnancy with a test or doctor visit
  2. Start prenatal vitamins with 400-800 mcg folic acid
  3. Schedule first prenatal appointment around week 8
  4. Avoid alcohol, smoking, and unsafe medications
  5. Limit caffeine to under 200mg/day

Second Trimester: Weeks 13-26

The "golden trimester." Most women feel their best during these weeks. Energy returns, nausea fades, and you may start showing. This is often the most enjoyable phase of pregnancy.

Fetal Development

  • Week 16: Baby can make facial expressions
  • Week 20: halfway point; you may feel first movements ("quickening")
  • Week 24: Lungs develop surfactant; baby has a chance of survival if born now
  • Week 26: Eyes open; baby responds to sounds and light

Maternal Changes

  • Baby bump: Uterus rises above the pelvis
  • Glow: Increased blood flow and oil production
  • Back pain: Center of gravity shifts forward
  • Round ligament pain: Sharp pains on the sides of your belly
  • Leg cramps: Especially at night

Key Actions

  1. Schedule anatomy scan ultrasound (around week 20)
  2. Start thinking about birth classes and pediatrician
  3. Consider maternity leave planning
  4. Continue moderate exercise (walking, swimming, prenatal yoga)
  5. Sleep on your left side to improve blood flow

Third Trimester: Weeks 27-40+

The final stretch. Your baby is growing rapidly, gaining weight, and positioning for birth. You may feel tired, uncomfortable, and eager for delivery—all completely normal.

Fetal Development

  • Week 28: Baby can blink and dream (REM sleep)
  • Week 32: Bones fully formed but still soft
  • Week 36: Baby drops into birth position (head down)
  • Week 37-42: Considered "term"; lungs are the last organs to mature

Maternal Changes

  • Shortness of breath: Uterus pushes up on diaphragm
  • Swelling: Feet, ankles, and hands may puff up
  • Braxton Hicks: Practice contractions become noticeable
  • Pelvic pressure: Baby's weight bears down on pelvis
  • Insomnia: Hard to get comfortable at night

Key Actions

  1. Pack hospital bag by week 35
  2. Install car seat and practice installing it
  3. Finalize birth plan with your provider
  4. Know the signs of labor: contractions, water breaking, bloody show
  5. Know when to call the doctor: decreased fetal movement, severe headache, vision changes

Due Date vs. Actual Delivery

Only about 4% of women deliver on their exact due date. Your due date is a 40-week estimate based on your last menstrual period. Most babies arrive between weeks 38 and 42. Your doctor will consider your pregnancy "post-term" if you go past 42 weeks and may discuss induction.

When to Call Your Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience:

  • Vaginal bleeding or spotting
  • Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  • Sudden severe headache or vision changes
  • Decreased fetal movement (especially in third trimester)
  • Fever over 100.4°F (38°C)
  • Painful urination or back pain
